# voice.md — StoryVaultFiles

## Who I am talking to
One person, alone, late at night, headphones on. They love a mystery they can't stop
thinking about, they've half-heard about the case, and they want the real story told
properly. Not the jump-scare version, and not the clickbait version.

## How the channel sounds
A calm, low, intimate documentary narrator (an AI voice; there is no on-camera host).
Short sentences for tension. Longer ones for atmosphere. Concrete details over adjectives:
the date, the time, the object on the table, the last words on the radio. Second person
is used sparingly ("Imagine standing on that deck…"). Silence and restraint do the work.

## Words I never use
"insane", "crazy", "mind-blowing", "you won't believe", "let's dive in", "smash that like
button", "without further ado", "spooky", "creepy" (show it, don't say it), exclamation
marks, emojis.

## Words and phrases that are mine
"This is case file number <N>." (said once, about 10–20 seconds in, after the cold open)
"The file remains open."
Chapter structure: setting → the event → the investigation → the theories → what remains unknown.

## What I will not claim
Anything not in the sources. Invented quotes or dialogue presented as real. That a living,
uncharged person did it. A theory stated as fact. Gore, or lingering on injuries. Content
centred on harm to children.

## My format
Faceless. 8–12 minute story videos with an AI narrator, stock footage, real case photos
and burned-in captions. No channel intro and no subscribe pitch before the end. One ask at
the close: a question for the comments, then one line to subscribe. Every video also
becomes a 45–60 second vertical Short that ends by pointing to the full case file.
